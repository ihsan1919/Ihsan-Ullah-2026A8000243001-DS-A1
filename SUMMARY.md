I audited the intuitionistic-fuzzy (IF) decision data of Roszkowska et al. (2021, Entropy 23:563, CC BY 4.0): satisfaction with ten urban services in 83 European cities, derived from the 2019 EU Perception Survey (Eurostat urb_percep). Each city-criterion cell is an IF value (μ satisfied, ν unsatisfied, π "don't know"). The testable question: can the published data reproduce the IF-TOPSIS ranking, and which comparisons survive uncertainty and measurement choices? I froze the transcribed tables with SHA-256 hashes and ran schema, range, missingness, duplicate, anomaly and leakage checks in an executable notebook (27 checks: 15 pass, 10 flags, 2 fails).

Positive result: all 83 published IFT scores reproduce within rounding (max error 0.0005), every μ+ν+π equals 1 within 0.0015, and all numeric claims in the text reproduce, including Kendall τ = 0.878. Two defects fail the audit. First, the illustrative decision matrix labelled London, Stockholm and Wien actually contains Aalborg, Amsterdam and Zurich values. Second, the published classic-TOPSIS scores match only if "don't know" answers are scored 0, not excluded as the paper states. This undermines the claimed IF-versus-classic gap.

Measurement counterexample: two cities with the same 80:20 satisfied ratio receive IFT 0.80 versus 0.68 when one has 25% "don't know". Rescoring on opinion-holders moves Rostock from rank 30 to 17. An optimistic multinomial bootstrap orders only 11 of 82 adjacent rank pairs with 95% support. Prohibited claims include "Zurich has Europe's best quality of life" and most "city X outranks city Y" statements. The Eurostat cross-check and the manual transcription spot-check are pending and logged.
